      How to organise your media folders
      
      
        
      SWARM scans one or more media roots — folders you point it at from the Settings tab. When you add a root you also pick its required asset type (Movies, TV shows, Music, or Photos & videos) so SWARM knows what to expect there. A root can't overlap another root, and you can run with more than one.
      
        
      Inside a root, follow the same layout Plex, Jellyfin, and Kodi use:
      
        
        
          
      • Movies — Movie Name (Year)/Movie Name (Year).mkv. Extras go in Featurettes/, Trailers/, Deleted Scenes/, etc. beside the movie.
        • 
          
      • TV — Show Name (Year)/Season 01/Show Name - S01E02.mkv. Multi-episode files (S01E02-E03) and a Specials/ season are recognised.
        • 
          
      • Music — Artist/Album/01 Track Title.flac. Disc folders (CD1/) are absorbed automatically.
        • 
          
      • Subtitles — a .srt/.vtt next to the video (optionally in a Subs/ folder), named after it, e.g. Movie Name (Year).en.srt.
        • 
        
      
        
      Older installations may retain a "Legacy mixed" root. New roots require one specific asset type. The Media roots info button on the Settings tab repeats this with more detail.
      
      
      
    
      

    
      
      Want the technical details?
      
      
        
      Every connection between your devices is end-to-end encrypted with TLS 1.3 — the same standard banks use — and mutually verified, so a device has to be one you've actually invited before it can ever connect.
      
        
      New devices join with a short one-time code you generate yourself. On your home network, a device can also be trusted directly with its own short-lived pairing code — nothing has to leave your network to do it.
      
        
      Signing in to generate codes needs a basic account (just an email and password) that's kept completely separate from your media — it stores nothing about your library, your files, or what you watch. Login credentials on your devices are kept in hardware-backed secure storage, not as plain files anything could read.
